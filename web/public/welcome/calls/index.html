Your voice
in the meeting.

Speechless plays into a virtual audio cable, the call app uses that cable as its microphone, and the other side hears you as if you were speaking. The cable is the one piece that is not in the app: it is a small system driver, and a web page cannot install one. Ten minutes, once.

Step 1

Install a virtual audio cable.

A virtual cable is a fake speaker wired to a fake microphone: whatever plays into the speaker comes out of the microphone. Each platform has one; only Windows lacks an open-source option, because Windows audio drivers must be signed with a paid certificate.

Windows VB-Cable (free, donationware; closed source but the standard choice). Run the installer as administrator and reboot. It adds CABLE Input as a speaker and CABLE Output as a microphone.
macOS BlackHole (open source, GPL). The 2-channel build is enough. It appears as BlackHole 2ch in both the output and input lists.
Linux Nothing to install. PipeWire and PulseAudio have a null sink built in: pactl load-module module-null-sink sink_name=speechless sink_properties=device.description=Speechless. The sink is the speaker; its monitor, Monitor of Speechless, is the microphone.
Step 2

Tick the cable in Speechless.

  1. Open the app in Chrome or Edge on the computer, then Settings → Devices.
  2. Tap Show names if the devices are unnamed. The browser asks for microphone access once; that is only so it may show the device names.
  3. Under Speak through, tick the cable's speaker side (CABLE Input, BlackHole 2ch or Speechless).
  4. Tick your own headphones as well, so you hear what the call hears.
Order matters The volume slider on the Voice tab applies to every ticked output. Above 100% boosts the voice, which helps when the call hears it quietly.
Step 3

Point the call app at the cable.

In the call app, choose the cable's microphone side as your microphone. Keep your normal speakers or headphones as the speaker.

Teams

Before a call: Settings → Devices → Microphone. During a call: the More (⋯) menu → Settings → Device settings. Turn off Noise suppression there, or Teams may clip the start of words.

Zoom

The arrow next to the Mute button → pick the cable under Select a microphone. In Audio settings set Suppress background noise to Low and turn off Automatically adjust microphone volume.

Google Meet

More options (⋮) → Settings → Audio → Microphone. Meet has no noise-suppression toggle on the free tier, so if words clip, raise the volume in Speechless instead.

Type something in Speechless and tap Speak. The call app's microphone meter should move, and you should hear it in your headphones. Nothing sounds? Check that you ticked the cable's speaker side in Speechless and chose its microphone side in the call app; they have different names.

Step 4

Let Speechless hear the call.

Quick replies need to know what the other person said. On a computer the browser cannot hear a call through the microphone, so Speechless captures what the computer plays instead.

  1. Add your ElevenLabs key on the Voice tab if you have not. Transcribing a call uses ElevenLabs Scribe, so this part needs a key and costs Scribe minutes.
  2. On the Devices tab set Listen with to What this computer plays (Teams, Zoom), via ElevenLabs.
  3. Back in the conversation, tap the microphone button. Chrome opens its share dialog: choose Entire screen and tick Also share system audio.
The screen is never recorded Chrome shows a "sharing your screen" bar for as long as Listen is on, because sharing the screen is the only way Chrome offers system audio. Speechless drops the video track the instant sharing starts. The only thing that leaves the computer is the audio of someone speaking, sent for transcription. Your own voice is muted out while it plays, so it is not transcribed as "them".
If it does not work

The usual suspects.

  • The cable is not in the list. Reboot after installing it, then reload the app. On macOS, allow the driver in System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  • The tick-boxes are greyed out. That browser cannot choose an output device. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer; Safari, Firefox and phones cannot do this.
  • The call hears nothing. The browser's own fallback voice always plays on the system default, not the cable. Check the badge in the app's header says ElevenLabs or Microsoft, not Browser voice.
  • Words are clipped at the start. Turn off noise suppression in the call app; it mistakes the sudden onset of a synthetic voice for noise.
  • It is too quiet. Raise the volume slider in Speechless above 100%, and turn off automatic microphone level adjustment in the call app.
  • Listen hears my own voice too. Expected briefly at the tail of a clip. If it keeps happening, untick your headphones and listen to the call through them from the call app only.

Set up once, then just type.

The settings stay on this computer. Next call, open the app and speak.
